🌟 Introduction to Units and Measurement

Welcome to this comprehensive exploration of Physical Quantities, Systems of Units, and Dimensional Analysis. This chapter forms the foundation of physics, enabling us to quantify and understand the physical world around us.

🎯 Why Study Units and Measurement?

Measurement is the foundation of experimental science and technology. The advancement of physics has always been closely tied to our ability to measure physical quantities with greater precision and accuracy.

📊 Physical Quantities

🔹 What are Physical Quantities?

A physical quantity is a property of a material or system that can be quantified by measurement. It consists of two parts:

  • Numerical Value (Magnitude) - How much?
  • Unit - In what measure?
Physical Quantity = Numerical Value × Unit
Example: Length = 5 meters (5 is magnitude, meter is unit)

🔹 Types of Physical Quantities

1️⃣ Fundamental Quantities

Definition: Quantities that are independent and cannot be derived from other quantities.

0

The Seven Fundamental Quantities:

  • 📏 Length - meter (m)
  • ⚖️ Mass - kilogram (kg)
  • ⏱️ Time - second (s)
  • ⚡ Electric Current - ampere (A)
  • 🌡️ Temperature - kelvin (K)
  • 💡 Luminous Intensity - candela (cd)
  • 🧪 Amount of Substance - mole (mol)

2️⃣ Derived Quantities

Definition: Quantities derived from fundamental quantities through mathematical relations.

∞

Common Examples:

  • 🏃 Velocity = Length / Time (m/s)
  • ⚡ Acceleration = Velocity / Time (m/s²)
  • 💪 Force = Mass × Acceleration (N)
  • ⚙️ Work = Force × Distance (J)
  • 🔋 Power = Work / Time (W)
  • 📐 Area = Length × Length (m²)

🔹 Scalars and Vectors

📊 Scalar Quantities

Quantities with magnitude only

  • Mass, Temperature, Speed
  • Distance, Energy, Work
  • Time, Volume, Density
Example: Temperature = 25°C

🎯 Vector Quantities

Quantities with magnitude and direction

  • Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration
  • Force, Momentum, Torque
  • Electric Field, Magnetic Field
Example: Velocity = 50 m/s North

🌍 International System of Units (SI Units)

The SI (Système International d'Unités) is the modern form of the metric system and is the most widely used system of measurement worldwide.

🎯 History

The SI system was established in 1960 at the 11th General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM) to provide a universal standard for measurements.

📋 SI Base Units Table

Physical Quantity SI Unit Symbol Definition

Mass Kilogram kg Defined by the Planck constant (h = 6.62607015 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s)

Electric Current Ampere A Defined by elementary charge (e = 1.602176634 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)
Temperature Kelvin K Defined by Boltzmann constant (k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K)
Luminous Intensity Candela cd Luminous intensity of source emitting monochromatic radiation
Amount of Substance Mole mol Defined by Avogadro constant (NA = 6.02214076 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)

🔬 Dimensional Analysis

📖 What is Dimensional Analysis?

Dimensional analysis is a powerful mathematical technique used in physics to check the correctness of equations, derive relationships between physical quantities, and convert units from one system to another.

🔹 Dimensions of Physical Quantities

The dimension of a physical quantity represents the nature of that quantity and how it depends on the base quantities.

Standard Notation:
[M] = Mass, [L] = Length, [T] = Time
[I] = Current, [K] = Temperature, [mol] = Amount, [cd] = Luminous Intensity

📊 Dimensions of Common Physical Quantities

Physical Quantity Formula Dimensional Formula SI Unit
Area Length × Width [M⁰L²T⁰] m²
Volume Length × Width × Height [M⁰L³T⁰] m³
Density Mass / Volume [ML⁻³T⁰] kg/m³
Velocity Displacement / Time [M⁰LT⁻¹] m/s
Acceleration Velocity / Time [M⁰LT⁻²] m/s²
Force Mass × Acceleration [MLT⁻²] N (Newton)
Momentum Mass × Velocity [MLT⁻¹] kg⋅m/s
Energy/Work Force × Distance [ML²T⁻²] J (Joule)
Power Energy / Time [ML²T⁻³] W (Watt)
Pressure Force / Area [ML⁻¹T⁻²] Pa (Pascal)
Frequency 1 / Time [M⁰L⁰T⁻¹] Hz (Hertz)
Charge Current × Time [M⁰L⁰T¹I¹] C (Coulomb)

🎯 Applications of Dimensional Analysis

1️⃣ Checking Dimensional Consistency

Verify if an equation is dimensionally correct by ensuring both sides have the same dimensions.

Example: s = ut + ½at²
[L] = [LT⁻¹][T] + [LT⁻²][T²]
[L] = [L] + [L] ✓ Correct!

2️⃣ Deriving Relationships

Derive possible relationships between physical quantities using dimensional analysis.

Example: Time period of pendulum
T ∝ l^a × m^b × g^c
T = 2π√(l/g)

3️⃣ Unit Conversion

Convert units from one system to another using dimensional formulas.

Example:
1 N = 1 kg⋅m/s²
= 10⁵ g⋅cm/s²
= 10⁵ dyne

📝 Principle of Homogeneity

Statement: In a correct physical equation, the dimensions of all terms on both sides must be the same.

Example: In v = u + at, all three terms [v], [u], and [at] have dimensions of [LT⁻¹]

3D Model: Principle of Homogeneity

⚠️ Limitations of Dimensional Analysis

  • ❌ Cannot determine dimensionless constants (like π, e, numerical factors)
  • ❌ Cannot derive equations involving more than one term (e.g., s = ut + ½at²)
  • ❌ Cannot determine whether a quantity is scalar or vector
  • ❌ Cannot be used for trigonometric, exponential, or logarithmic functions
  • ❌ Fails when physical quantity depends on more than three variables

🔍 Worked Examples

Example 1: Checking Dimensional Consistency

Question: Check if the equation E = mc² is dimensionally correct.

Solution:

LHS: Energy [E] = [ML²T⁻²]
RHS: [m][c²] = [M][LT⁻¹]² = [M][L²T⁻²] = [ML²T⁻²]
LHS = RHS ✓ Dimensionally correct!

Example 2: Deriving Formula

Question: The time period T of a simple pendulum depends on length l and acceleration due to gravity g. Derive the relationship.

Solution:

Let T ∝ l^a × g^b
[M⁰L⁰T¹] = [L]^a × [LT⁻²]^b
[M⁰L⁰T¹] = [L^(a+b)T^(-2b)]
Comparing: a + b = 0, -2b = 1
∴ b = -1/2, a = 1/2
T ∝ √(l/g)

Example 3: Unit Conversion

Question: Convert 1 joule into erg.

Solution:

1 J = 1 kg⋅m²⋅s⁻²
= (1000 g)⋅(100 cm)²⋅s⁻²
= 1000 × 10000 × g⋅cm²⋅s⁻²
= 10⁷ erg

📖 Quick Reference Formulas

Important Formulas

  • Velocity: v = Δx/Δt
  • Acceleration: a = Δv/Δt
  • Force: F = ma
  • Work: W = F⋅d
  • Power: P = W/t
  • Density: ρ = m/V
  • Pressure: P = F/A
  • Energy: E = mc²

Conversion Factors

  • 1 km = 1000 m
  • 1 hour = 3600 s
  • 1 N = 10⁵ dyne
  • 1 J = 10⁷ erg
  • 1 W = 10⁷ erg/s
  • 1 Pa = 10 dyne/cm²
  • 1 kg = 1000 g
  • 1 L = 10⁻³ m³

Physical Constants

  • c = 3×10⁸ m/s
  • G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg²
  • h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ J⋅s
  • k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K
  • NA = 6.022×10²³ /mol
  • e = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ C
  • g = 9.8 m/s²
  • R = 8.314 J/(mol⋅K)
